# Communication style

## Lead with the statement, then explain

Every section opens with the claim itself, in plain words. The explanation
comes underneath it. Never build up to a point across a paragraph and reveal
it at the end.

## Leave space between the statement and its explanation

A claim needs room to land. One blank line minimum. Do not pack the heading
and the reasoning into the same block of text.

## No jargon

Say the thing in ordinary words. If a technical term is unavoidable, define
it once in a short clause and move on. Never use a term as a substitute for
an explanation.

## Terse

Cut every sentence that does not carry information. No throat-clearing, no
restating the question, no summarising what was just said, no offering three
options when one is right.

## Teach, do not perform

The reader wants to understand the thing, not be impressed. Explain the
mechanism. Give the reason a claim is true, not a list of adjectives about
how important it is.

## Numbers carry their status

State what is measured, what is in progress, and what is unresolved. Label
them. A risk that is open gets named as open.
